SOLD: Six Flags Unloads Seven Parks

January 14, 2007

Six Flags has finally found a buyer for seven of their parks, in a deal that will fetch them $312 million, helping their debt situation. The CNL property company will be the new owner and will lease the parks to the PARC operating company. The sale included Six Flags Darien Lake in New York, Six Flags Elitch Gardens in Denver, Water World Concord in Northern California, Frontier City and the White Water Bay water park in Oklahoma City, Splashtown in Houston, and Wild Waves and Enchanted Village near Seattle. The deal is expected to be finalized within the next few months. Both Elitch Gardens and Darien Lake will no longer carry the Six Flags name once the sale is finalized.

According to a Six Flags spokesperson, the park chain is not "actively seeking" a buyer for Six Flags Magic Mountain.

Six Flags over Georgia "Free Day" Chaos

April 5, 2007

It was not a day to remember for Six Flags over Georgia officials, as everything seemed to unravel. The day was supposed to be a free day, where anyone who showed up would get in for free. A local radio station had bought out the park for the promotion, but didn't really think it through. When there are free tickets to be had, people will come in droves.

Many free ticket seekers arrived at the park around 5 a.m. to find the freeway jammed with traffic. Getting off the freeway was yet another step toward getting into the park. Six Flags employees passed out free tickets in front of the park, which led to fights breaking out over tickets. At 9 a.m., when the park finally opened its gates, 15,000 people jammed in quickly. At that point the park had reached capacity and the gates were closed, leaving thousands of people locked out.

This whole incident really makes me think about a similar promotion coming up at Six Flags Discovery Kingdom. During spring break, the park will offer free admission to the first 1,000 people. It'll be interesting to see if Discovery Kingdom changes their plans at all in the wake of the chaos at Six Flags over Georgia.

Six Flags to Purchase Discovery Kingdom

June 6, 2007

Photo pending upload — SFDK entrance sign

Six Flags' CEO Mark Shapiro has announced that Six Flags Discovery Kingdom will soon become officially owned by Six Flags. Since the park came to Vallejo, California in the 1980s, the city has had ownership of the park. Shapiro has made it clear that Six Flags intends to purchase the park this year.

Six Flags Discovery Kingdom, formerly Marine World, has been run by Six Flags since 1997. In the ten years they've operated the park, Six Flags has had an operational contract giving them a cut of the profits. In 2006, the city of Vallejo took in around seven million dollars while Six Flags took in just six million. Six Flags estimates the purchase would cost around 52 million dollars and be complete by the end of July.

The option for Six Flags to purchase Discovery Kingdom was set to end in 2010. Six Flags had interest in purchasing the park for a few years but was never able to due to their massive debt. With the park chain selling off seven parks last year, they now have the financial flexibility to purchase Discovery Kingdom. Even with $16 million worth of improvements to the park this year, I'm looking forward to seeing what Six Flags does with this unique park.

Six Flags Discovery Kingdom will also be expanding their list of kid lands with the addition of Thomas Town, opening June 20th. The most noteworthy comment from the company's conference call was that Thomas Town will be coming to Magic Mountain in 2008.

It's Official: Six Flags Now Owns Discovery Kingdom

August 2, 2007

It's official — Six Flags now officially owns Discovery Kingdom in Vallejo, California. On July 31st, Six Flags paid a lump sum of $55 million to the city of Vallejo. While many still claim this sale won't affect the park, you still have to believe Six Flags will be willing to invest more money into a park they now own.

Prior to the sale, the city and Six Flags had a revenue sharing agreement. While the city's cut is expected to be substantially smaller, Vallejo is still expected to earn around $750,000 in park admission taxes per year. The sale will also cut the city's debt in half.

City Manager Joe Tanner made a last ditch effort to exempt the elephants from the sale, but fell short. Attorneys advised Tanner that removing the elephants could cause legal problems. Tanner was quoted as saying, "If you take elephants out of the deal, you just bought the elephants and you'd pay for the potential profit from those elephants to the park. How can I put the city in such harm's way?"

Universal Studios Hollywood Fire

June 2, 2008

On the morning of June 1st, a large fire broke out on the back lot of Universal Studios Hollywood. Many structures were destroyed, including the sound stage which housed the Studio Tour's King Kong attraction.

Universal released a statement noting that no NBC Universal employees were hurt, though some firefighters sustained minor injuries. While Universal Studios Hollywood and CityWalk were closed to the public on Sunday to allow firefighters access, the MTV Movie Awards were held as planned that evening at the Gibson Amphitheater. The park and CityWalk reopened for normal operations Monday morning.

The fire was contained to one section of the backlot. New York Street and the King Kong attraction were lost, but the iconic courthouse on Courthouse Square and half of the buildings facing the square were saved. A video vault containing thousands of video and digital copies of films and TV shows was also affected, though most items are believed to be replaceable. None of the 30 soundstages on the lot were damaged.
