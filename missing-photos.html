Disneyland

August 9, 2007

Welcome to Disneyland, Walt Disney's original Magic Kingdom.

To my surprise, the horse drawn carriages were in operation. It's the first time I've seen these guys in years.

The other horse making their way down Main Street.

The new attraction at the park for 2007 is Finding Nemo's Submarine Voyage.

A look at the queue were most people spend two hours. Luckily we got there early enough were we only had to wait 20 minutes.

The Matterhorn towers above the submarine lagoon.

A look at the loading docks for Finding Nemo.

The Voyager and the Mariner stand by to be put into service.

The hatch is open and guests descend into the subs.

Of course the whole ride would be terrible if they didn't include these little guys. "Mine!"

Ah yes, the Disneyland Monorail, a Mechanical Engeneering Landmark. And in the background is the monorail themed to be the new subs...NEMOMORAIL!

Over in New Orleans Square, the Disney Gallery has officially closed its doors.

A construction wall has been placed near the Pirates of the Caribbean queue. As you can see, there is already some building debris.

The gallery is closed, yet they still have banners promoting it...strange.

Now onto the newly re-themed Tom Sawyer's Island: Pirate's Lair.

The new show on the island was just finishing up as we made our way towards the island.

Some of the new themeing included this pirate who would just not let go.

Even after being dragged from the depths, he still held onto the chest.

The caves had many new props and lighting effects. Davy Jones could be heard all throughout the caves.

A Pirate band can be found playing in front of the old mill.

Night time rolled around as we made our way for New Orleans Square train station.

The Ward Kimball makes its way toward the platform.

Before we left, we took a look at Space Mountain with its beautiful lighting setup.

A final look at Disneyland as we strolled through Tomorrowland.

Universal Studios Hollywood

August 14, 2007

Welcome back for another Universal Studios Hollywood update.

First stop was Back to the Future: The Ride. A 30 minute wait was the shortest all day.

A display is set up as the count down continues towards the Back to the Future: The Ride closure.

Last ride, Labor Day 2007!

The Starway escalator now has posters including this one for Jurassic Park: The Ride.

Revenge of the Mummy is now more shocking than ever. And honestly, I thought the new version was not an improvement. I personally felt the old version of the finale was better.

The House of Horrors is scaring visitors all year long.

And of course Back to the Future is closing this Labor Day.

When we got down to the Lower Lot the first ride we hit was Jurassic Park.

This year Jurassic Park is "Wetter Than Ever."

New water cannons shoot off just a boat comes down the drop.

Then the boat splashes down...

...and the riders are completely soaked. Overall, the "Wetter Than Ever" change to Jurassic Park is a huge improvement for those hot summer days.

Elsewhere on the Lower Lot, a steel drum band was playing.

Next on the agenda was a trip on Universal's world famous Studio Tour.

A new addition to the Studio Tour is the Fast and the Furious demo.

King Kong's Skull Island is also new since the last time I took the tour.

While we couldn't go into the town square from Back to the Future due to filming, I did notice the Lyon Estates entrance.

Down Wisteria Lane where they were staging a house.

An interesting looking set was being worked on near "The Great Outdoors" cabin.

On the Upper Lot, the Simpsons were quickly rushed away as the Paparazzi swarmed.

After a full day of fun at the park, I can say that Universal really is the Entertainment Capital of LA. While they may not pack in tons of coasters, it's still a very entertaining park with a vast array of attractions.

A look at the fountain near the entrance as we depart the park.

The last bit of sunlight disappeared as we enjoyed our time at City Walk. Thanks for viewing this Universal Studios Hollywood update!

Great America

August 26, 2007

Welcome to the first TPV update from Great America here in Northern California.

First up was Drop Zone.

Survivor was down for a good part of the morning before finally opening.

After lunch, we checked out Boomerang Bay.

Over at Great Barrier Reef, the new height enforcements that have been implemented since the drowning are quite evident.

Everyone is now forced to go past a lifeguard with a height pole, similar to on a coaster.

Next, the group decided to check out the show in the Nicktoons area. To our surprise, we saw the final run of this show. Next year a new show is supposed to take its place.

It's the theme park version of Where's Waldo! Can you find my friend?

And for the finale, not one, but TWO people got slimed.

Call me crazy, but I actually enjoyed Grizzly! So much, I went on it twice! OMG!

I was really surprised at how nice the landscaping is in some areas. This lady was so not into the car ride.

Another odd thing, Great America put enough effort into changing the names on all the Gilroy/Bonfonte Gardens signs, but didn't even change the Paramount sign at the entrance to the parking lot!

I leave you with this shot, which looks just like Knott's Berry Farm.
